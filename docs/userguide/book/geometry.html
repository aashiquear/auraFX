auraFX User Guide

Geometry and meshing

auraFX builds solid geometry from short commands: primitives, booleans, outlines extruded, revolved or swept, and imported surfaces. Each body keeps named faces, so later steps can refer to the top of a plate or the side of a hole. It also makes structured hexahedral meshes: boxes, and boxes of matrix holding inclusions whose surfaces the elements follow exactly. The meshes carry element blocks, side sets and node sets, check their quality, and write Exodus, Abaqus and Gmsh files for solvers. Plates with holes, dog-bone specimens and swept surfaces have their own generators, and bricks and cylinders built with commands can be meshed with mesh body commands.

The Geometry source

Add it with Sources › Geometry. Its Properties show:

  • Commands: the command list, edited in the box and applied with Apply. The Insert menus under the box (Shape, Outline, Combine, Move, Mesh, Names and sets) add a command with example numbers at the end, each with a line saying what it does, so the commands need not be remembered: insert, change the numbers, Apply.
  • From geometry to a mesh: the six steps as a checklist with a button each. Mesh every body writes the mesh command, sized to a twentieth of the model, and shows the hexahedra; Surface / Hex mesh / Tet mesh switch what is shown; Mesh Quality adds the filter that finds distorted elements; Save data… writes the mesh with its blocks and sets for a solver. A tick appears as each step is done. Model › New geometry adds the source and opens here.
  • Bodies: a tree of the bodies the commands make. It shows how each body was built and lists its faces with their numbers and names. A mistake in a command shows here, with the line it is on.
  • Resolution: cells along the longest side of each body when its surface is drawn.
  • Output: Surface gives the bodies’ surfaces. Distance field gives the signed distance to the nearest body on a grid (negative inside), with the body and face nearest each point.

The surface output carries the cell arrays BodyId and FaceId. Its field arrays are FaceNames (indexed by face number), BodyIds, BodyNames, and each body’s Volume and SurfaceArea. Color by FaceId to see the faces.

Commands

Write one command per line, or separate commands with ;. Text after # is a comment. Bodies, outlines and faces are numbered from 1 in the order they are made. Sizes are in model units and angles in degrees.

All primitives are centred on the origin, and round ones stand along z.

Outlines and profile solids

An outline is a closed 2-D curve. Build it, then extrude, revolve or sweep it.

CommandDoes
outline 1 polyline 0 0 2 0 2 1 0 1straight edges through the points, closed back to the start

outline 1 circle radius 1 center 0 0a circle
outline 1 ellipse x 2 y 1an ellipse
outline 1 regular sides 6 radius 1a regular polygon

Faces of profile solids are named after the outline’s curves: edge 1, edge 2, … for straight edges, then arc 1, fillet 1, circle, spline 1. Extrusions add bottom and top, partial revolutions start and end, and sweeps start and end.

A face keeps its name through booleans, so the faces of a hole are still cylinder 2 side after the cylinder is subtracted, unless you rename them.

Webcuts make blocks you can sweep. A brick cut along one of its own axes (even moved or turned) gives two bricks, so both stay structured, and their meshes meet node to node on the cut, which becomes an internal surface with a side set of its own. Cut a long part into a coarse and a fine region this way and imprint them so the sizes agree. Other cuts (a slanted plane, a cylinder, another body) make general pieces, meshed together on one overlay grid. When a plane runs exactly through a row of grid-cell centres, the overlay gives those cells to the first body.

Meshing bodies

These commands make a hexahedral mesh, shown when the Geometry source’s Output is Hex mesh:

CommandDoes
mesh body 1 2 size 0.1 element HEX20meshes bodies with elements of about that size (HEX8 by default)
mesh body 3 size 0.1 scheme overlaychooses how: structured, overlay or stairstep (by default bricks and cylinders are structured, everything else overlay)
mesh body 3 size 0.1 scheme mappedone structured block bent onto a body that a box can be bent into (a ball, a rounded block, a superquadric): the box’s outer nodes go onto the body along rays from its centre, the inner ones follow (with Winslow smoothing); takes intervals and bias too
mesh body 1 size 0.1 intervals 20 8 4 bias 3 1 1 cluster starta structured body’s pieces along its own x, y and z (a cylinder: a quarter of the way round, across the radius, along the height) instead of from the size, and per axis the largest piece over the smallest, the small ones at the start, end or both
block 7 add body 2 name 'rod'puts bodies in an element block (by default each body is its own block, with its id and name)
sideset 1 add face 6 9 name 'top'a side set of the mesh faces on those geometry faces
nodeset 2 add face 1 name 'left'a node set of the nodes on those faces

Bricks become structured boxes, and cylinders become an O-grid disk swept along their axis, each moved, turned and scaled like its body. Every geometry face also becomes a side set named after it, such as brick 1 x+ or cylinder 2 side. With HEX20 or HEX27 elements, the new nodes on a cylinder’s side lie on the exact surface. Bodies meshed together are welded where their nodes coincide, and the faces between two bodies’ elements form a side set and a node set named interface <body> - <body> (for structured and overlay bodies alike).

Every other body (spheres, booleans, outlines, sweeps, imported surfaces) is meshed on an overlay grid; see Overlay meshing below.

  • One grid. All overlay bodies share one grid of the smallest size asked for, so bodies that touch meet node to node. Each body is its own block, and the faces between two bodies form a side set named interface <body> - <body>.
  • Overlap. Where bodies overlap, the one listed first in the mesh commands keeps the overlap.
  • Curved elements. With HEX20 or HEX27 elements, the new nodes on a body’s surface are moved onto it.
  • Stair-step. scheme stairstep keeps whole grid cells, for a quick look.

Python

afx.mesh.geometry() builds geometry command by command. Each command is checked at once, so a mistake raises an error naming the line.

g = afx.mesh.geometry()
g.cmd("brick x 10 y 5 z 2")
g.cmd("cylinder radius 1 height 4; subtract body 2 from body 1")
for b in g.bodies():                  # id, name, history, faces, bounds
    print(b["id"], b["name"], b["faces"])
part = g.result(resolution=96)        # adds the Geometry source to the pipeline
print(g.journal())                    # every command so far

The Geometry source is also an ordinary source: afx.geometry(commands="sphere radius 1"). Edits made in the command box are recorded in the trace (Trace in the Python panel), so the journal can be replayed.

Signed Distance Field

Filters › Mesh › Signed Distance Field turns a closed surface, such as an STL file or the Geometry output, into an image grid with a Distance point array. The array is negative inside. Resolution sets the cells along the surface’s longest side, and Margin adds space around it as a fraction of its size. Contour it at 0 to get the surface back, or threshold it to keep the inside.

d = afx.load("part.stl").signed_distance(resolution=128, margin=0.1)

Meshes, blocks and sets

A generated mesh is an unstructured grid of hexahedra with the cell array ElementBlock (the block each element belongs to) and field arrays for the rest: ElementBlockIds and ElementBlockNames, one SideSet <name> per side set (element index and Exodus side number 1 to 6 per face) and one NodeSet <name> per node set (node indices). Exodus files keep all of them, as do Abaqus files (element sets, node sets and surfaces). Gmsh files keep the blocks and side sets as physical groups. Exodus files written elsewhere read back the same way, with their side sets and node sets.

Filters › Mesh › Extract Set shows one set: a side set’s faces as a surface (with the owning element’s block and element variables) or a node set’s nodes as points.

walls = mesh.extract_set(set="x-")
mesh.save("part.e")          # or part.inp, part.msh

Box Mesh

Sources › Box Mesh makes a box of hexahedra: its Size, Origin and Cells along x, y and z, optionally Graded. The grading is the ratio of the largest to the smallest interval along each axis, with the small intervals at the Start, the End or Both ends. It has one block, block_1, and side sets and node sets x-, x+, y-, y+, z- and z+ on its faces. Element gives HEX8, HEX20 or HEX27 elements.

b = afx.box_mesh(size=(2, 1, 1), cells=(40, 20, 20), grading=(3, 1, 1), cluster="Start")

Inclusion Mesh

Sources › Inclusion Mesh makes a box of matrix with randomly placed inclusions, meshed with conforming hexahedra. This is the method of hexaform, a structured mesher written by the author of auraFX:

  • Background grid. A grid of cubic cells covers the box. Cells along the shortest side sets the cell size.
  • Packing. Each inclusion gets a box of whole grid cells. The boxes are placed at random, largest first, at least Cells between boxes apart and Cells from the walls away from the box faces. So anything that is placed can be meshed.
  • Blocks. Each inclusion box is filled with a block:
    • a core filled from its six sides;
    • layers out to the exact inclusion surface, where every node lies on the surface;
    • layers out to the flat box faces, whose nodes are the grid’s own.
  • Welding. The pieces are welded exactly, so there are no hanging nodes and the elements fill the box exactly.

Packing Random places the inclusions once each, largest first. Collective then keeps moving the placed ones, each by a random step or a push towards free space, to open room for the rest. This reaches denser packings, but takes longer. Shape chooses spheres, ellipsoids or superquadrics (with Semi-axes / radius and the superquadric Exponents), or harmonic grains with a random surface of the given Roughness. Radius and Radius spread set the size distribution, and Random orientation turns the non-spherical shapes. Seed fixes the placement.

The mesh has:

  • block 1 matrix (the grid and the outer layers) and block 100 inclusions, or one block per inclusion with One block per inclusion;
  • side sets x- … z+ on the box faces and interface (the matrix side of every inclusion surface), and node sets x- … z+;
  • the element variable particle_id: the inclusion’s number inside it, minus that number in its outer layers, 0 elsewhere;
  • field arrays InclusionsPlaced, InclusionsRequested, VolumeFraction and CellSize.

With Element HEX20 or HEX27, the new nodes on the inclusion surfaces are placed on the exact surface too. Periodic pairs every node on a box face with the node opposite it (matched by their other two coordinates). It adds node sets x-_node_set/x+_node_set (and y, z) listing the pairs in the same order, and the field arrays PeriodicPairs x (and y, z), for periodic boundary conditions.

The advanced settings shape the blocks:

  • the number of layers inside and outside the surface, and their grading;
  • the core size and roundness;
  • the box size relative to the inclusion;
  • the spacing of the nodes on the surface.

The default blocks have a smallest scaled Jacobian of 1/√3 = 0.577, at the corners of the inclusion boxes.

m = afx.inclusion_mesh(size=(1, 1, 1), cells=24, count=8, radius=0.075, shape="Sphere", seed=7)
m.save("rve.e")

Plate Mesh

Sources › Plate Mesh makes a plate of Cell size hexahedra, one element through the thickness. The width and height must be whole multiples of the cell size. It can have a circular or elliptical through-hole, given by its semi-axes a and b, the ellipse’s angle and its centre (empty for the plate centre).

The hole is meshed by a ring of elements whose inner nodes lie on the exact curve and whose outer edge is a rectangle of whole background cells. So the ring hands over to the plate without transition elements. The ring’s layers grow from about the hole’s arc spacing to the cell size.

The mesh has:

  • blocks 1 plate and 2 ring;
  • side sets and node sets x- … z+;
  • node sets outer (the four edges) and grips (both x faces);
  • a node set and a side set hole.
p = afx.plate_mesh(size=(100, 50, 1), cell=2.5, hole="Circle", a=2.5)

Dog-bone Mesh

Sources › Dog-bone Mesh makes a flat tensile specimen as one mapped block:

  • Planform. Grips of Grip width at both ends and a gage of Gage width and Gage length in the middle, joined by circular fillets of Fillet radius.
  • Thickness. Constant in the grips. Along the gage it follows a spline through the Thickness stations (pairs of position from the gage start and thickness), and it blends linearly across the fillets.
  • Cells. Cell size in the gage, growing to Grip cell size in the grips, with Layers through the thickness layers.

The mesh has:

  • blocks 1 gage, 2 shoulder and 3 grip;
  • side sets and node sets x- and x+;
  • node sets grips, z-, z+, edge-, edge+, gage- and gage+, and station_1, … for each thickness station.
d = afx.dogbone_mesh(stations=[0, 0.37, 4, 0.35, 8, 0.346], layers=2)

Sweep Quads

Filters › Mesh › Sweep Quads sweeps a surface of quadrilaterals into layers of hexahedra along a Direction over a Distance. The layers can be graded like Box Mesh’s cells. Blocks come from the surface’s ElementBlock array. The side sets are bottom (the swept surface), top and side (swept from the surface’s boundary edges), with node sets of the same names. Any quadrilateral surface can be swept, such as one face of a mesh from Extract Set or a surface read from a file.

thick = plate.extract_set(set="z-").sweep_quads(direction=(0, 0, 1), distance=4, layers=3)

Face Mesh: triangles and quadrilaterals on a planar face

Sources › Face Mesh meshes a flat face drawn with the outline commands (see Outlines and profile solids): an Outer outline with Holes cut out (outline numbers), at a Size, with Quadrilaterals (the default) or Triangles:

  • The outlines are divided at the size, corners kept. Pieces per outline fixes the number of pieces of each outline instead (outer first).
  • Size at the holes and Growth refine near the holes: the size there grows by Growth per unit of distance until it reaches Size. Smallest size and Largest size limit it.
  • Triangles are made by Delaunay triangulation and improved (long edges split, short ones removed, edges swapped, nodes smoothed). Quadrilaterals come from triangles of twice the size: pairs of triangles that make good quads are joined, then every quad is cut into four and every triangle left into three, so the face is all quads.
  • The cell array Quality is each quad’s scaled Jacobian (a triangle’s: its smallest angle over 60°), and MinQuality in the field data the worst.

A plate 4 × 2 with a hole of radius 0.5 at size 0.15 gives roughly its area over the size squared in quads (about 320), the worst scaled Jacobian above 0.3; swept 3 layers through 0.5 with Sweep Quads it is 3 hexahedra per quad, all with scaled Jacobians above 0.3.

face = afx.face_mesh(commands="outline 1 rectangle x 4 y 2 center 2 1\noutline 2 circle radius 0.5 center 2 1",
                     outer=1, holes="2", size=0.15, near=0.05, growth=0.3)
plate = face.sweep_quads(direction=(0, 0, 1), distance=0.5, layers=3)

Remesh Surface

Filters › Mesh › Remesh Surface meshes any triangle surface again (from a file, Extract Surface or a Geometry source), with Triangles or Quadrilaterals:

  • Size (0: the input’s mean edge length), or Size from a point array (the size wanted at each point, such as a Calculator result), or Curvature: chord tolerance, which makes the size small where the surface curves so that the mesh stays within the tolerance of it (a chord of length 2 √(2 R t) on a radius R).
  • Edges where the surface turns by more than Sharp edge angle, and open boundaries, are kept; the other nodes slide along the surface and are put back onto it.

A sphere of radius 0.5 remeshed at 0.05 has edges within 20 % of 0.05; with a chord tolerance of 0.002 its edges are close to 0.089.

r = part.remesh_surface(size=0.05)
c = part.remesh_surface(size=1, tolerance=0.002, element="Quadrilaterals")

Mesh sets, merging and mixed meshes

  • Filters › Mesh › Define Mesh Set adds a Side set (the boundary faces whose nodes all satisfy an expression) or a Node set (the nodes that do), named, from an expression of coordsX, coordsY, coordsZ and the point arrays, like the Calculator: abs(coordsZ - 1) < 1e-6 or coordsX*coordsX + coordsY*coordsY < 0.25.
  • Merge Nodes joins nodes closer than a tolerance (meshes made apart that touch), renumbering the cells and node sets; MergedNodes in the field data says how many went.
  • Tetrahedra in a Region turns the hexahedra all of whose nodes satisfy an expression into tetrahedra. Each gets a node at its centre: across a face between two converted hexahedra there are four tetrahedra, and on a face against a hexahedron that stays (or on the boundary) a pyramid, so the mixed mesh is conforming. The volume is unchanged; cell arrays follow, and a side on a converted hexahedron moves to its pyramid’s base.
  • To move, turn or scale a mesh, use Transform: blocks and sets stay with it.
m = afx.box_mesh(size=(1, 1, 1), cells=(8, 8, 8))
m = m.define_mesh_set(kind="Side set", name="hot", expression="coordsZ > 1 - 1e-6")
mixed = m.hex_to_tets(expression="coordsX < 0.5")

Overlay meshing

Overlay meshing fills any shape with hexahedra. It works in five steps:

  1. Grid. A grid of cubes is laid over the shape, and each cell takes the material found at most of its sample points.

  2. Clean-up. Pieces smaller than Smallest piece, and cells that stick out or form layers one cell thick, join their surroundings. Around every grid node, each material and the rest must each be connected through faces, and no material may form a saddle (faces pointing both ways along an axis). Single cells are changed until this holds, so the next step always has room.

  3. Pillowing. Each material is wrapped in a layer of elements. No element then has more than one face on a surface.

  4. Surfaces. The nodes on each surface move onto the true surface. Where two faces of the geometry meet at a convex edge, the nodes nearest the edge are put on it, and on its corners. For imported surfaces, the faces are the patches between creases of more than 30°. Where the shape is only known as voxels, the nodes are smoothed along the surface instead.

  5. Smoothing. The rest of the mesh is smoothed in stages:

    • Laplacian smoothing;
    • small moves of the nodes of elements whose scaled Jacobian is below 0.6;
    • a wider search below 0.3;
    • untangling of anything still flat.

    Every move is kept only if the elements around it stay valid.

The result has:

  • one block per material;
  • side sets and node sets for the outer faces of the grid (x- … z+), for each surface of the model (named after the geometry face, or surface <block>), and for each interface between two blocks (interface <a> - <b>);
  • field data MinScaledJacobian and InvertedElements, to check the quality at a glance.

Filters › Mesh › Overlay Mesh runs it on data:

  • Image data with a label array (FeatureIds by default): each label becomes a block, named grain N for grain numbers. Label 0 is empty space. Cell size 0 meshes on the image’s own voxels.
  • A closed surface (STL, OBJ, PLY, …): the solid inside it. Cell size 0 uses a thirtieth of its largest size. Each patch between creases gets a side set (surface 1, surface 2, …), and Keep sharp edges puts nodes on the creases and corners.
  • Interfaces Smooth runs all five steps; Stair-step keeps whole cells, every element a perfect cube.
  • Mesh the empty space meshes label 0, or the space around a surface, as a block of its own (void).
  • Periodic keeps the outer faces of the grid flat and unmoved, and pairs their nodes across opposite faces (PeriodicPairs x/y/z), for representative volume elements.
mesh = ebsd_volume.overlay_mesh(array="FeatureIds", periodic=True)
part = afx.load("part.stl").overlay_mesh(cell=0.5)

Tetrahedral meshes

The Geometry source’s Output Tet mesh fills every body named in a mesh body command with tetrahedra of the size asked for. Each body is a block, and each geometry face a side set (with those of sideset and nodeset commands). Filters › Mesh › Tet Mesh does the same for a closed surface such as an STL file, with a side set per patch of the surface between creases.

The tetrahedra are made in three steps:

  1. Lattice. A regular lattice of tetrahedra, from cubes and their centres, is laid over the shape.
  2. Snapping. Lattice nodes close to the surface are moved onto it, so no element is cut thin.
  3. Cutting. The tetrahedra the surface crosses are cut, and their inside parts split into tetrahedra.

Every element is positive and the mesh is conforming. The smallest dihedral angle stays around 20 degrees or more (above 21 on the test shapes). The volume converges to the shape’s as the element size shrinks: a sphere meshed with elements a tenth of its radius is within 0.3 %.

part = afx.geometry(output="Tet mesh", commands="sphere radius 1; mesh body 1 size 0.1")
tets = afx.load("part.stl").tet_mesh(cell=0.5)

Refine Region

Filters › Mesh › Refine Region refines part of a tetrahedral mesh:

  • Which elements. The tetrahedra whose centre lies in the box from Box min to Box max, or whose value of a chosen cell array reaches is at least.
  • How. Each of them splits into eight.
  • Neighbours. A neighbour that shares split edges gets a centre point joined to its faces, so the mesh stays conforming. This gives finer elements where they are needed.
  • Sets. Side sets and node sets follow.

Refine Mesh

Filters › Mesh › Refine Mesh splits every element into eight, Levels times.

  • Hexahedra (HEX8) split at their edge midpoints, face centres and centre.
  • Tetrahedra keep their four corners and split the inner octahedron along its shortest diagonal.
  • New nodes are shared between neighbours, so the mesh stays conforming.
  • Blocks and element variables carry over. Each side-set face becomes its four sub-faces, and a node set gains the new nodes that lie on it.

Convert Elements

Filters › Mesh › Convert Elements changes the element kind, keeping blocks and sets:

  • Hexahedra to tetrahedra cuts each face of a hexahedron into two triangles, along the diagonal through its lowest-numbered node so that neighbouring hexahedra agree. Each triangle is joined to the centre, giving 12 tetrahedra per hexahedron.
  • Tetrahedra to hexahedra gives 4 hexahedra per tetrahedron, one at each corner. They meet at the edge midpoints, face centres and centre.

Smooth Mesh

Filters › Mesh › Smooth Mesh moves the inner nodes of a hexahedral or tetrahedral mesh. Nodes on its boundary, and those of an optional node set, stay put.

  • Smart Laplacian (the default) moves each node to the mean of its neighbours, keeping the move only when the worst element around it does not get worse.
  • Laplacian always moves; on a badly shaped domain it can fold elements.
  • Condition number searches around each node for the position that lowers the condition numbers of its elements. It is slower but untangles better.
  • Winslow moves each node whose eight hexahedra are arranged like a structured grid to the solution of the Winslow (elliptic) equations over its 3 × 3 × 3 block of nodes, which spreads the nodes evenly without folding; other nodes use Smart Laplacian. A grid whose inner nodes were moved by up to a third of a cell comes back to within 5 % of where they were.

The output’s field data MinScaledJacobian gives the worst element after smoothing.

Boundary Layers

Filters › Mesh › Boundary Layers inserts Layers thin layers of hexahedra under the faces of a side set, for wall-resolved flow or contact.

  • Thickness. The first layer is First layer thickness thick, and each next one Growth ratio times thicker.
  • Placement. The faces stay where they are, and the mesh behind them is pushed inwards by the total thickness. At the edge of the side set, the layers slide along the neighbouring boundary faces.
  • Sets. The side set moves to the outer layer, and the layers’ side faces join the side sets beside them. If the layers are thicker than the elements behind them would allow, the filter refuses rather than fold elements.
fine = mesh.refine_mesh(levels=1)
tets = mesh.convert_elements(to="Hexahedra to tetrahedra")
walls = mesh.boundary_layers(side_set="z+", layers=4, first=0.01, growth=1.3)
smooth = tets.smooth_mesh(scheme="Smart Laplacian", iterations=10)

Grains on meshes

Assign Grains

Filters › Mesh › Assign Grains gives every element of a mesh a grain, as the cell array grain_id, and that grain’s orientation, as EulerAngles (radians). Grains from sets where the grains come from:

  • Voronoi: Grains seeds placed at random in the mesh’s box. Each element takes the grain of the seed nearest its centre. Size spread above 0 makes Laguerre grains of different sizes. Orientations are random or a fibre. The seeds are kept as GrainSeeds.
  • Nearest point: a second input of points, or a table with x, y and z columns, carrying grain ids (Grain id array, grain_id by default). Each element takes the grain of the point nearest its centre. If the points have orientations, as EulerAngles in radians or phi1, Phi and phi2 columns in degrees, the grain takes them.
  • From a volume: a second input that is a microstructure, for example an EBSD volume or a synthetic one, with FeatureIds. Each element takes the feature of the cell its centre lies in, with that feature’s mean orientation.

One block per grain makes each grain an element block named grain N. Side set on grain boundaries adds a side set, and a node set, named grain boundaries on the faces between elements of different grains. Grains without an orientation in the source get a random one. The grains’ orientations are also kept as the field array GrainEulerAngles, one row per grain id.

Mesh grains from a microstructure. Overlay Mesh on a volume with FeatureIds and orientations already gives one block per grain. Each element also gets its grain’s grain_id and mean orientation.

Orientation files. Saving a mesh with grain orientations as .tex writes one line per grain, in order of grain id: φ1 Φ φ2 in degrees. Crystal-plasticity input decks read orientations in this form.

grains = mesh.assign_grains(grains=80, blocks=True, boundaries=True)
from_points = mesh.assign_grains(afx.load("points.csv"), method="Nearest point")
grains.save("specimen.e")
grains.save("specimen.tex")

Worked example: geometry to a solver-ready polycrystal

import aurafx as afx
part = afx.geometry(output="Hex mesh", commands='''
brick x 4 y 1 z 0.5
cylinder radius 0.2 height 1
subtract body 2 from body 1
mesh body 1 size 0.05 scheme overlay
''')
poly = part.assign_grains(grains=200, blocks=True, boundaries=True)
q = poly.mesh_quality(output="Summary table")       # check the elements
poly.save("plate.e")                                  # blocks per grain, side sets per face
poly.save("plate.tex")                                # one orientation per grain

The Exodus file holds:

  • one block per grain;
  • the plate’s faces as side sets (brick 1 x-, cylinder 2 side, …);
  • the grain boundaries as a side set.

The .tex file lists the grains’ orientations, ready for a crystal-plasticity input deck.

The case templates of Running simulations write and run the solver’s input from the grain mesh directly, here or on a remote machine, and open the results when the run ends:

jid = afx.run_case(poly, "moose", "~/runs/plate", host="cluster", np=32,
                   material="Nickel", axis="x", strain=0.02, steps=100)

On the results, per-grain statistics come from the grain blocks (MOOSE) or from Assign Grains with the case’s point file (PINNACLE).

Elevate

Filters › Mesh › Elevate turns 8-node hexahedra into 20-node (edge midpoints) or 27-node (also face and cell centres) ones. Neighbouring elements share their new nodes, and the blocks, sets and element variables are kept. The new nodes lie at the midpoints. Inclusion Mesh’s own Element setting also puts those on the inclusion surfaces onto the surface.

Limits

  • Surfaces are drawn by contouring each body’s distance field, so sharp edges are rounded over about one cell. Raise Resolution for crisper edges. The Volume and SurfaceArea of smooth bodies are within about 0.5 % at a resolution of 96.
  • The distances of superquadrics, harmonic shapes, partial revolutions, sweeps and non-uniform scaling are approximate away from the surface. The surface itself is exact.
  • Sweeps along sharply bent paths join the pieces with a mitre, not a rounded bend.
  • Imported surfaces must be closed. Their inside is found from the direction the triangles face.
  • Inclusions must be star-shaped (one surface point along every direction from their centre) and cannot touch: each lies in its own box of grid cells, which caps how dense a packing can get even with collective packing.
  • Assign Grains places a grain by each element’s centre: grain boundaries follow the element faces (a finer mesh resolves them better), and the tetrahedral and hexahedral side sets need the mesh to be all one kind.
  • Tetrahedral meshes have one element size, refined locally with Refine Region; they round sharp edges over about an element, and touching bodies are meshed separately (their nodes do not match). Local refinement works on tetrahedra only (hexahedra are refined everywhere, or turned into tetrahedra in a region).
  • Overlay meshes keep convex edges and corners. Concave edges, such as where two crossed cylinders join, are rounded over about a cell, and so are features thinner than a cell.
  • The clean-up may give a few cells at grain boundaries to the neighbouring grain, so very small grains lose some volume: a voxel sphere 5 voxels in radius comes out about 8 % smaller than the exact sphere.
  • Overlay meshing of 100 000 voxels takes about 3 seconds.
  • Gmsh files carry no node sets.
  • A loft’s rims (where its side meets the ends) come out rough in hexahedral meshes, on the overlay grid and with the mapped scheme. The mapped scheme also makes sharp edges that do not follow the box’s edges wavy: use it for rounded bodies.
  • The mapped scheme needs a body that every ray from the centre of its box leaves once (it refuses a ring). On a smooth body such as a ball, the eight corner elements of the single block are nearly flat (scaled Jacobian about 0.1); a rounded block keeps them above 0.3.
  • Face Mesh meshes flat faces; curved faces are meshed with Remesh Surface from a surface of the part. Remesh Surface keeps the nodes of sharp edges where they are (it adds nodes along them but does not remove any). On a faceted surface, such as a contoured Geometry surface, a few quadrilaterals come out poorly shaped (on a torus about 0.5 % below a scaled Jacobian of 0.2): check the Quality array.
