auraFX User Guide

CAD modeling

auraFX has a parametric part modeler. A part is a short list of features (a sketch extruded, a hole cut, a boss added) with named dimensions. Change a dimension and the whole part rebuilds, and everything built on it (meshes, grains, solver cases) can be re-run. The part keeps named faces, so a later step can say “the wall of the bore” instead of a number that changes when the part does.

It sits beside the command-based Geometry source of the previous chapter. Use Geometry for quick specimens written as text; use a CAD model when the part has dimensions you want to change, sketches, patterns and holes.

The CAD Model source

Add one with Sources › CAD Model (or the command palette, Ctrl+K). It starts as a plate. Its Properties show:

  • Dimensions: the part’s named dimensions. Type a new value or an expression such as width / 2 and press Enter; the part and everything downstream update. The value the expression gives is shown next to it. reset returns to the value stored in the part. A dimension changed here belongs to this node and leaves the part untouched, so two nodes can show two sizes of one part.
  • Configuration: shown when the part has configurations (named sets of dimensions such as small and large); pick one to build that variant.
  • Features: the part’s history in order. The checkbox suppresses a feature (the part is rebuilt without it).
  • Faces: every face of the result with its number and name, its volume and area.
  • Resolution and Output: cells along the longest side when the surface is drawn, and Surface or Distance field (the signed distance to the part, negative inside).

Faces and their names

The surface has the cell arrays BodyId and FaceId, and the field arrays FaceNames (indexed by face number), BodyNames, Volume and SurfaceArea: the same layout as the Geometry source, so everything that works on a Geometry result works on a CAD model. Color by FaceId to see the faces.

A face is named after the feature that made it and its place on that feature: a brick has x-, x+, y-, y+, z- and z+; a cylinder or a hole has side, bottom and top; an extruded sketch has bottom, top and one side N for each line, arc or circle of the sketch (the sketch’s own order). Bore side is the wall of a hole named Bore; Base z+ is the top of a brick named Base. Rename a feature and its faces are renamed with it, which the later downstream check uses to follow them.

The CAD environment

Editing happens in one mode of the same window. Open it from Sources › CAD Model… (which adds a new part and opens it), from Edit in CAD… (right-click a CAD Model in the Pipeline, or the button at the top of its Properties), or from Python with part.edit_cad(). While it is open:

  • the toolbar becomes the ribbon (every button with an icon and its name) with tabs Sketch, Features, Modify, Inspect, Assemble and Files (Alt+1 to Alt+6). Each button adds a feature with sensible sizes at the top of the part; you then set its numbers in the feature panel. Inspect reads the part (volume, surface, size, center, mass for a density, the area of a cross-section); Assemble places other parts (see below); Files starts from a template or opens and saves a document.
  • the Pipeline panel becomes the Model tree: the part’s dimensions, its features in order (the checkbox suppresses one; double-click renames; right-click for Suppress, Roll back to here, Move up, Move down, Delete), the modifiers on the whole part and the configurations: named sets of dimension values, variants of one part. Type a name and press Save values to keep the dimensions’ present values under it (saving under an existing name replaces it), click a configuration to make it the active one, × removes it. The parts of an assembly are listed under Components: click one to move or turn it, double-click to rename it.
  • the Properties panel becomes the Feature panel: every field of the selected feature. Each number accepts an expression of the dimensions: type width / 2 instead of a number and the field follows the dimension (the value it gives is shown beside it). Choices (the end condition of an extrusion, the type of a hole, a mirror plane) are menus. With no feature selected it shows the part itself and its faces.
  • the timeline bar at the bottom lists the features in order. The orange marker is the rollback marker: only the features before it are built, so you can look at the part as it was after the third feature, or insert a hole earlier in the history: a feature added while rolled back goes in at the marker. ◀ ▶ move it. Apply always hands over the whole part.
  • the menu bar gains a CAD menu, and the part is built again after every edit, in the background (the timeline shows the time it took and the part’s volume).
  • the pipeline is paused. Other 3D views, charts and spreadsheets stay as they were and say so in a grey banner; nothing downstream re-runs on every edit.

Finding your way

You do not need to know where a command is:

  • Next, at the top of the model tree, follows what you are doing and offers the steps that make sense now as buttons: on an empty part Sketch on the Top plane, Box and Start from a template; in a sketch it says whether the outline is closed and how many degrees of freedom are left, and offers Extrude; when the part does not build it shows the reason and Undo. The same sentence is in the status bar at the bottom of the window.
  • Command search: press S in the 3D view (or Ctrl+K, or the Search button beside the tabs). A small box opens at the pointer; type a few letters (fil, hole, export) and press Enter. Each result says which tab it is on, so you can find the button next time.
  • Right-click a face of the part in the 3D view: its name, kind and area, and what can be done with it (Sketch on this face, Fillet edges…, Shell, Pattern holes, Mirror, Select its feature). Right-click a feature in the tree or the timeline for Suppress, Roll back to here and the rest.
  • Hover over any button for what it does; a greyed button says why it cannot be used yet (Apply when the part does not build, a constraint without the right selection, Extrude without a closed outline).
  • A feature that stops the part from building is red with a warning sign in the tree, and the reason stays at the top of the feature panel, with an Undo the last change button, until the part builds again. A feature marked ≈ is shown roughly by the kernel in use.
  • Keys: Alt+1…6 the tabs, Ctrl+Enter Apply, Esc leave, Delete and F2 on the selected feature, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo (inside an open sketch they undo the sketch’s own changes); in a sketch L line, R rectangle, C circle, A arc, P point.

While you edit, the part is rebuilt after every change. With the node’s Kernel on Auto the quick distance-field kernel does that, unless the part has features only the exact kernel can show (fillets, chamfers and drafts on picked edges or faces, shells with picked open faces): then the exact B-rep kernel builds it, which can take a few seconds, and a feature it could only approximate is marked ≈. A kernel chosen on the node (B-rep or Implicit) is used as it is. Apply always builds with the node’s setting.

Starter parts. Files › New from template (or afx.cad_model(template="flange")) replaces the part by one of six starters, all driven by named dimensions (the enclosure follows width, depth, height and wall; the flange its diameter, bolt circle and number of holes):

The right end of the ribbon leaves the environment:

  • Apply hands the part to the CAD Model node and closes the environment; the nodes below it re-run. A part that does not build is refused, with the reason.
  • Apply, keep editing does the same and stays.
  • Discard (or Esc, which asks) closes it and leaves everything as it was.

All the edits of a session are one undo step in the main window (Edit › Undo after Apply takes back the whole session). Inside the environment, Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y, the timeline bar, the CAD menu) step through single edits.

The Inspect tab reads the part: volume, surface, size, center of volume, mass for a density, and the area of a cross-section at a height. The Files tab starts from a template, opens an .afxcad file, or saves the part as one.

From Python:

e = part.edit_cad()            # open the environment; e is part.cad
e.box(size=(10, 10, 6), name="Boss", at=(0, 0, 11))
e.evaluate()                   # build it now (the window does this by itself)
e.rollback(1)                  # build only the first feature
e.undo(); e.redo()
e.apply()                      # or e.apply(keep_editing=True) / e.discard()

What the nodes below depend on

Other nodes refer to a part by the names inside it: an Extract Set that names Bore side, a mesh command that names a face, a clip plane placed inside the plate. Editing the part can break these. So Apply checks them first. It compares the part as the nodes below were built against with the part you made, finds what they refer to that changed, and opens a dialog:

  • What changed in the part lists faces that were renamed, replaced, removed or moved, dimensions added or removed, and the new bounds and volume.
  • Each row is something below the part that refers to a changed thing: the object, its parameter, what happened to the thing it refers to (renamed to…, removed, now outside the part, one of several), the default fix, the assistant’s fix when you asked for one, and a choice per row (default, assistant, leave).
  • The default fixes are the safe ones. A renamed face is renamed where it is used, including inside command and expression text (whole names only: Bore side is not found inside Bore sides). A face that is gone but has one successor in the same place is replaced by it, and marked (check) because that is a guess. A position that fell outside the part is moved onto its nearest point. A face that is simply gone, or has several possible successors, has no default: you choose, or ask the assistant.
  • Ask the assistant sends the changes, the conflicts and each affected node’s filter and parameters to the assistant, which answers with a list of changes; they appear in the Assistant’s fix column and the same chat shows its reasoning.
  • Apply fixes and re-run applies the part and your choices, together, as one undo step; Apply without fixing applies the part and leaves the references (those nodes will show their own errors); Back to CAD returns; Discard CAD changes closes the environment as it was. The checkbox re-run the nodes below now postpones their re-run: they keep their old results, marked by a banner with Run now.

When nothing below is affected the dialog is only the question Re-run N downstream filters? with Run now, Later and Back to CAD. A Python filter’s code is searched for the names as text: such rows say (in code) and have no default fix, because the name may not be a reference.

From Python:

e = part.edit_cad()
e.rename("Bore", "Pin hole")
report = e.check()             # {'diff': ..., 'conflicts': [...], 'downstream': [...], 'prompt': ...}
e.apply()                      # fix="default": applies the default fixes
e.apply(fix="none")            # leave what the check found as it is
e.apply(fix=answer_text)       # the text of an assistant's answer (a JSON list of fixes)
e.apply(run="later")           # the nodes below wait; part.cad.run_held() runs them

A part from a Geometry source or a surface

Edit in CAD… is not only for CAD Models. Right-click a Geometry source, or any object whose surface is closed (a sphere, a clipped and capped part, the surface of a mesh), in the Pipeline panel and choose Edit in CAD… (also Model › Edit in CAD… for the selected object). A new CAD Model is made whose first feature is that solid as an imported body: its triangles are kept in the part, fixed, and every feature of the environment adds to it or cuts from it: a hole, a pocket sketched on the Top plane, a rib. A Geometry source with several bodies gives one imported body per body, named as the bodies are. The object you started from is hidden, not changed; the new part does not follow it afterwards.

g = afx.mesh.geometry()
g.cmd("brick x 30 y 20 z 10")
body = g.result()
c = afx.cad_from(body, name="Bracket")      # a CAD Model; its first feature is the brick
c.cad.hole(diameter=6, depth=30, at=(0, 0, 6), name="Bore")
e = c.edit_cad()                            # or carry on in the window

The surface must be closed (every edge shared by two triangles): an open one is refused with the number of open edges; close it first (Clip Closed Surface, or take the surface of a volume). Up to 400 000 triangles are kept (Decimate a finer surface first). An imported body is triangles, not exact faces: its whole surface is one face named after the body (body 1 surface), while the features you add have their usual faces (Bore side). The exact kernel does not cut or join a triangle body, so once features are added the part is built by the implicit kernel, and the node’s kernel note says so.

Sketching

A sketch is a drawing on a plane that a feature extrudes or revolves. Start one from the ribbon’s Sketch tab:

  • Top (XY), Front (XZ) or Right (YZ), or Top of the part (the plane of the highest face, to add a boss or cut a pocket).
  • A face… opens the face picker: click any flat face of the part in the 3D view (or tick it in the list) and press Apply. Slanted faces work too: the sketch lies on the face, its x axis along the world’s x (or y) laid flat on it. With no feature selected, Faces in the Feature panel also has Sketch here next to each flat face. A sketch started on a face stays on that face when the part changes.
  • A reference plane (each one has a button in the tab), made with Plane….

To change an existing feature’s sketch, right-click it in the model tree and choose Edit sketch…, or press Edit sketch… in its properties.

Reference planes

Plane… opens a form in the feature panel that makes a named plane, kept with the part:

  • From a plane: Top, Front, Right or another reference plane, moved by an Offset along its normal and turned by an Angle about its own x or y axis.
  • From a face: a flat face of the part, moved and turned the same way.
  • Through 3 points: the normal follows the right-hand rule from point 1 to 2 to 3, and the sketch’s x axis runs from point 1 to point 2.

The planes show as dashed squares with their names in the 3D view, and are listed under Reference planes in the feature panel (no feature selected) with Sketch and × (remove; sketches already drawn on a plane keep their place). A sketch on a plane 20 above the top plane, turned 30° about its x axis, extrudes along the turned normal in both kernels.

m.cad.plane(name="Tilted", from_plane="XY", offset=20, angle=30, about="x")
m.cad.plane(name="P", points=[(0, 0, 0), (10, 0, 0), (0, 10, 5)])
m.cad.extrude(m.cad.sketch(plane="Tilted").rectangle(20, 10), depth=5)

The canvas takes the place of the 3D view while a sketch is open:

  • Draw with the tools in the ribbon: Line (connected lines; right-click ends the chain), Rectangle (two corners), Circle (center, then a point on it), Arc (center, start, end), Spline, Point. A line drawn nearly horizontal or vertical becomes so.
  • Snap (the ribbon’s toggle) places new points on what is under the cursor, shown by an orange mark and its name: existing points (which joins shapes), centers of circles and arcs, midpoints of lines, quadrants of circles, intersections, the nearest point on curves, and the grid. While drawing, dashed guide lines show when the cursor lines up horizontally or vertically with a point, also one far away or the last point of the shape, and the point lands on that line. Each kind is switched on or off under Snap in the Feature panel, with the grid step; hold Ctrl to place a point freely. A point placed on a midpoint, a curve or an intersection is constrained there (midpoint, on line, on circle).
  • Select shapes and points by clicking (Shift adds); drag a point to move it. Right or middle mouse drag pans, the wheel zooms, Esc ends a chain, Del deletes.
  • Constrain the selection with the ribbon: horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, equal, tangent, concentric, coincident, midpoint, on line, on circle, symmetric, fix. Dimension it: Length of a line, Radius, Diameter, Distance between two points (also Horizontal distance and Vertical distance), Distance to line (a point and a line), Distance between lines (two parallel lines) and Angle between two lines.
  • A dimension starts at the current size, with its value open for typing: type a number, or an expression of the part’s dimensions (width / 2, 3 in), and press Enter; the sketch follows the dimension from then on. Dimensions are drawn with extension lines and arrows (an arc for an angle, a leader for a radius or diameter). Drag a label to move it where it reads best, and double-click it to change the value. The Constraints list in the feature panel edits the same values.

  • The solver runs after every change. Shapes are blue while the sketch can still move (the status says how many degrees of freedom are left), white or black when it is fully defined, and red when constraints conflict (remove one). Construction turns the selected shapes into guides that are not part of the outline.

Finish from the feature panel: Extrude (or Cut, for a pocket) with a depth that may be an expression, reverse to go against the plane’s normal, Revolve about the plane’s vertical axis (draw on the Front plane with x as the radius), or Apply sketch when you edited the sketch of an existing feature. The sketch then becomes a feature with named faces: side 1, side 2… follow the order the shapes were drawn.

From Python, e.sketch_new(plane="XY", items=[...]) and e.sketch_edit("Bracket") open the canvas in the window, and e.sketch(...) with e.extrude(...) builds the same features without it.

Sketches that stay on their face

A sketch started on a face remembers the face by its name (Plate z+). Every time the part is built, the sketch plane is put back on that face as the features before it leave it: make the plate thicker and the boss drawn on its top moves up with it; a face that turns takes the sketch with it. The Feature panel says On the face … under the feature’s sketch, and the sketch canvas says so while you draw. A boss on a face grows out of the part and a cut goes into it.

If the face is gone (its feature was removed, or renamed, so no face has that name any more), the feature is marked in red in the model tree and the part does not build; the message names the feature and the face. Right-click the feature › Pick another face… lists the flat faces of the part as it is before that feature (click one in the list, Apply), and Detach from its face leaves the sketch where it last was, no longer following anything. A sketch on a principal plane or a reference plane can be given a face the same way (Put its sketch on a face…).

m.cad.box(size=(40, 30, "t"), at=(0, 0, "t / 2"), name="Plate")
boss = m.cad.sketch(face="Plate z+").circle(r=5)       # on a face, by its name
m.cad.extrude(boss, depth=5, name="Boss")              # grows out of the plate
m.set(t=12)                                            # the boss follows the top face
m.cad.sketch_face("Boss", "Plate x+")                  # another face for the same sketch
m.cad.sketch_face("Boss", None)                        # let go of the face

Side faces keep their names

Every line, arc and circle of a sketch has a number of its own, and the faces an extrusion or a revolve makes from them are named by it: Base side 3 is the face of entity 3 for as long as that entity exists. Adding a line gives it a new number (side 7), deleting one retires its number, and the others keep theirs, so a filter below the part that names Base side 3 still means the same face after the outline gained or lost a line. Files written before entities had numbers get them when they are opened, in the order that gives every face the name it had.

Sketches with constraints from Python

The sketch object of m.cad.sketch() draws line by line and takes the same constraints and dimensions as the canvas. Each drawing call returns what it drew; a line has .start and .end, a circle .center, and passing one of those points to the next call shares the corner:

m.cad.parameter("w", 40)
s = m.cad.sketch(plane="XY")
a = s.line((0, 0), (38, 1))            # drawn roughly ...
b = s.line(a.end, (41, 19))
c = s.line(b.end, (2, 22))
d = s.line(c.end, a.start)
s.fix(a.start)
s.horizontal(a).vertical(b).horizontal(c).vertical(d)   # ... squared up by constraints
s.distance(a, "w")                     # a length that follows the dimension w
s.dimension(a.end, b.end, 20)          # the distance between two points
print(s.solve())                       # {'status': 'fully defined', 'dof': 0, ...}
m.cad.extrude(s, depth=5, name="Plate")

The ready shapes (rectangle, circle, polygon, regular, and path(points) for an open line) can be mixed into the same sketch. extrude, revolve, sketch_add, sweep and loft solve the sketch themselves; solve() is for checking it first. A sketch whose constraints contradict each other is refused when it is used, with the size of the contradiction.

Sweeps and lofts

A sweep moves a closed outline along a path; a loft joins closed outlines drawn on parallel planes. Both are made from sketches kept with the part:

  1. On the Sketch tab draw the outline and, instead of extruding it, press Keep as a sketch (in the panel on the right). The sketch appears under Sketches in the model tree.
  2. Draw and keep the second sketch: for a sweep an open path (lines and arcs end to end, not closed) on a plane the profile’s plane crosses, starting where the profile is; for a loft another closed outline on a parallel plane at another height (set Offset before you draw).
  3. On the Features tab press Sweep or Loft. A button that is off says, when you point at it, what is still missing (a path, a second section, sections on one plane).

The feature panel of a sweep has profile and path (each a list of the kept sketches that fit, with Edit… to open the sketch), twist in degrees over the whole path, and Operation (Add or Cut: a swept cut makes a channel). A loft lists its sections with × to take one out and Add a section…. Changing a kept sketch (click it under Sketches, change it, Apply sketch) rebuilds every sweep and loft that uses it, and its dimensions may follow the part’s dimensions like any sketch’s. The faces are named Handle start, Handle end and Handle side; Vase bottom, Vase top and Vase side.

h = afx.cad_model(name="Handle")
h.cad.clear("Handle")
h.cad.sketch_add(h.cad.sketch(plane="YZ").circle(r=3), name="Profile")       # looks along x
h.cad.sketch_add(h.cad.sketch(plane="XY").path([(0, 0), (40, 0), (40, 25)]), name="Path")
h.cad.sweep("Profile", "Path", name="Handle")                                # op="cut" for a channel
h.cad.loft([h.cad.sketch(offset=-12).rectangle(20, 20),                      # sketches given in place
            h.cad.sketch(offset=-3).circle(r=4)], name="Foot")               # are kept as "Foot section"
h.cad.sketches()                                                             # name, plane, outlines, paths

Both kernels build them: the exact kernel a loft and a sweep along a flat path without twist; a twisted sweep, or one along a path that leaves its plane, is built by the implicit kernel (the node’s kernel note says why). The profile of a sweep is taken as it is seen along the start of the path, so draw it on a plane the path leaves at right angles; a profile lying along the path is refused. The sections of a loft must be on parallel planes at different heights.

Kernels, STEP and other files

A part is built by one of two kernels, chosen in the CAD Model’s Properties (Kernel):

  • B-rep builds exact solids with true faces and edges (a cylinder is a cylinder, a hole’s wall is a real cylindrical face). It maps primitives, extruded and revolved sketches, lofts, holes, patterns (as copies), rounding and hollowing of a feature or of the whole part, the rounded junction a feature’s blend makes with the part, drafts, mirrors about a face, combined parts, and cap chamfers. This is what STEP and IGES are written from, and it reads imported solids.
  • Implicit describes the part as a distance field. It builds every feature, including threads, sweeps and a loft’s shell, and never fails; edges are not exact. It leaves a picked fillet or a draft undone and says so.
  • Auto (the default) uses B-rep when it can build the part and Implicit otherwise. The result carries a field Kernel (which one built it) and KernelNotes (why B-rep could not).

Both kernels name faces the same way (Base z+, Bore side), so everything built on the names works on either. The CAD environment shows the part with the Implicit kernel while you edit (it is quick), and the node builds it with the kernel you chose when you apply.

Files. File › Open (and afx.load) opens a .step / .stp (AP203, AP214, AP242), .igs / .iges, .3mf, a SolidCraft .sc design, or an .afxcad document as a CAD Model: the file’s solid becomes the base feature of a part, with its bytes kept inside the document, so you can add features to it (cut a hole through an imported bracket) and the part still opens on another computer. STL and OBJ keep their ordinary readers; to edit one, add it as a CAD Model from Python.

m = afx.cad_model("bracket.step")                 # a solid from a STEP file as a CAD Model
m.cad.hole(diameter=6, depth=20, at=(10, 5, 8))   # cut into it
m.cad.save("bracket2.step")                       # exact STEP (faces and edges)
m.cad.save("bracket2.3mf"); m.cad.save("part.sc") # 3MF, a SolidCraft design (also .stl, .obj)
m.cad.save("bracket2.igs")                        # IGES, exact (the exact kernel only)
r = m.cad.save("lofted.step")                     # {"exact": False, "note": "faceted: ..."} when
                                                  # only the Implicit kernel builds the part

A part that only the Implicit kernel builds is written as STEP or STL from its tessellation: a valid solid of triangles whose header says faceted B-rep and why. IGES is written only for a part the exact kernel builds (its faces are true surfaces); for any other part the message says to write STEP instead.

The files auraFX writes were checked with an independent CAD reader: the same number of faces and the same volume, to a thousandth, for STEP and IGES (the checks are in the test suite and need that reader installed to run).

Edges, faces and measures

With the exact kernel the part has real edges, and you can work on them. In the Modify tab:

While one of these pickers is open you can also click the part in the 3D view: a click chooses the face under the pointer, or, for an edge, the edge of that face the click is nearest to (click again to un-choose). Chosen edges are drawn in orange and chosen faces are marked with a dot.

  • Fillet edges… and Chamfer edges… open a picker in the feature panel that lists the part’s edges, each with the two faces it lies between (Base z+ × Bore side), its length and whether it is curved. Tick the edges, set the size (a number or an expression of the dimensions) and press Apply: a Fillet or Chamfer feature is added after the features it modifies, so it follows them when you roll back or change a dimension.
  • Shell, open faces… lists the faces; tick the ones to remove (none: a closed hollow body) and give the wall.
  • Draft faces… lists the faces; tick the side faces to taper and give the angle. They stay fixed where the part meets the bottom plane and lean by that angle above it.
  • Pattern on a face… lists the flat faces; pick one, choose Holes or Bosses and a Row, Grid or Circle, and give the size, the count, the spacing (or the radius of the circle). The first copy sits at the face’s center and the row runs along the face’s own x axis, so the pattern follows a face that is not horizontal. A pattern on a face is one feature: the count and the spacing can be changed afterwards, and a hole’s copies share its face names (Hole side).
  • Mirror about a face… lists the flat faces; the reflection of the part about the chosen face is joined to it. Model half a part, mirror it, and the halves stay in step when a dimension changes.

These features need the B-rep kernel for a fillet, a chamfer and a draft. On the Implicit kernel a picked fillet or chamfer leaves the edges sharp and a draft leaves the faces vertical, and the result says so (KernelNotes); a picked-face shell is approximated. Patterns on a face and mirrors work on both kernels. Fillet and Chamfer without a picker round every edge of the selected feature or of the whole part.

The Inspect tab and Python read the part: mass (volume, surface, center, bounds, mass for a density), the distance between two parallel flat faces and the angle between two faces (in the tab: choose the two faces in the lists after between and press Distance), and the volume two parts overlap when one is placed against the other. A sketch-extruded feature names its faces top, bottom and side 1, side 2… in the order its outline was drawn (the default plate: Base side 1 at the front, then counter-clockwise); right-click a face in the 3D view to read its name.

b = afx.cad_model()                                  # the default plate
b.cad.edges()                                        # [{'index': 0, 'point': [...], 'length': 30.0,
                                                     #   'faces': ['Base bottom', 'Base side 1'], ...}, ...]
b.cad.fillet_edges(("Base top", "Base side 2"), size=2)  # the edge between two named faces
b.cad.chamfer_edges([0, 3, 5], size=1)               # edges by index (or a point on each)
b.cad.shell_faces(["Base top"], thickness=2)         # an open box
b.cad.draft(["Base side 1"], angle=5)                # taper a face, fixed at the bottom plane
b.cad.mirror_about("Base side 4", (1, 0, 0))         # the reflection about that face (the one at x = -20)
b.cad.edit("face_pattern", face="Base top", layout="circular", count=6, radius=12, diameter=4, depth=6)
b.cad.edit("combine", template="flange", op="subtract", at=(0, 0, 4))   # cut another part from this one
b.cad.mass(7.85)                                     # steel
b.cad.measure_faces("Base side 4", "Base side 2")    # {'parallel': True, 'distance': 40.0, ...}
b.cad.interference(other, at=(12, 6, 0))             # overlap volume with another CAD Model placed there

Assemblies

A CAD Model can hold several parts side by side. On the Assemble tab, Insert part places another part: a template, a blank part, or a CAD file (.afxcad, STEP, IGES, 3MF, .sc). The part keeps its own features and dimensions; the model shows all the parts together, as one surface with one body per part, and a part’s faces are named Part/Face (Lid/Base z+), so the mesh tools and the downstream check refer to them as they do to any face.

In the feature tree a Components section lists the parts. Tick one off to leave it out, click a name to see where it sits: three positions (mm) and three turns (degrees, about x, y and then z), which you can drag or type; × removes it. Check fit lists the pairs of parts that overlap, with the overlap volume in mm³ (the parts are compared two at a time, on the exact solids when the exact kernel builds them), and says so when none do.

Editing a part of the assembly

Double-click a part under Components (or right-click it › Edit component) to work on its own features. The model tree, the feature panel, the ribbon and the sketch canvas then belong to that part: the tree starts with a breadcrumb, Assembly › Part, and shows the part’s dimensions, sketches and features; every button adds to this part; faces are picked on it. The part is shown alone, in its own coordinates (where it was modelled, not where the assembly places it), and the rest of the assembly is hidden until you click the assembly’s name in the breadcrumb (or Back to the assembly on the Assemble tab, or in the command search). The edits are edits of the assembly: one undo history, and Apply hands over the whole assembly. The timeline’s rollback marker is the assembly’s and rests while a part is edited.

e = m.edit_cad()
e.edit_component("Cap")                    # every call now acts on the Cap's own part
e.hole(diameter=8, depth=30, at=(0, 0, 10), name="Bore")
e.extrude(e.sketch(face="Slab z+").circle(r=3, center=(12, 12)), depth=4, name="Boss")
e.edit_component(None)                     # back to the assembly

Mates and motion

A mate ties two parts together by a face of each, and the assembly works out where the parts go. On the Assemble tab press Add mate…, click a face on one part and a face on another (or tick them in the list, where each says flat or round), choose the kind and press Apply:

MateFacesWhat it does
coincidenttwo flatthe faces meet in one plane, looking at each other; flush has them look the same way
concentrictwo round (a hole, a pin)the two axes become one
distancetwo flat (or two round)parallel, that many mm apart
angletwo flat (or two round)the angle between them in degrees: 0 as a coincident mate leaves them, 90 at right angles
fixeda partthe part stays where it is placed: right-click it under Components › Fix in place

The mates are solved every time the assembly is built, each part moving as little as it can from where it is placed, so a part keeps the freedom its mates leave it. The Mates section of the model tree lists them and says how many ways the mated parts can still move (fully held at none); the position and turn shown for a part under Components is where it starts from, with where the mates put it underneath. A mate that cannot be met (its face is gone, the faces are the wrong kind, or it contradicts another mate) is marked in red with the reason; the rest of the assembly still builds. A mate that contradicts the others is refused when you make it. Fix one part first: with nothing fixed, both parts of a mate move to meet each other.

Motion: every distance and angle mate has a slider in the model tree; dragging it moves the assembly as it is rebuilt (the parts are built once and only placed again, so this is quick), and a drag is one step to undo. For an animation, let the mate follow a dimension of the assembly and key that dimension:

e = m.edit_cad()
e.parameter("gap", 5)
e.mate("fixed", "Base")
e.mate("coincident", "Base/Slab z+", "Cap/Slab z-")
r = e.mate("distance", "Base/Slab x+", "Cap/Slab x-", value="gap", name="Gap")
print(r["dof"], r["status"])               # 1 '1 way(s) left to move'
e.mate_set("Gap", value=12)                # moves the cap
e.mates(); e.placements()                  # each mate with its error, where each part is put
e.apply()
m.key("gap", 0, 5); m.key("gap", 1, 40)    # the dimension on the animation: the cap slides

Faces are written Part/Face (e.faces() lists them); a face of the assembly’s own part is just Face, and XY, XZ, YZ are the assembly’s planes (e.mate("distance", "XY", "Cap/Slab z-", value=7)). While an assembly has mates it is shown by the exact kernel where that builds the parts, because mates read the planes and axes of the faces.

Add part, Cut with part and Keep common part (on the same tab) do something else: they make another part a feature of this one, which joins it, removes its volume or keeps only the common volume. Use components for parts that stay apart (a bolt in a bracket) and these for parts that become one solid.

m = afx.cad_model(name="Housing")
m.cad.component_add(name="Lid", template="bracket", at=(0, 0, 14))
m.cad.component_add(name="Bolt", file="bolt.step", at=(20, 0, 14), rotate=(0, 90, 0))
m.cad.component_op("Lid", "add", kind="cylinder", radius=3, height=6)   # one edit of a part in place
m.cad.component_move("Bolt", by=(0, 5, 0))
m.cad.component_interference()                        # [{'a': 'Lid', 'b': 'Bolt', 'volume': 120.4}]
m.cad.components()                                    # name, position, turn, visibility

From a CAD part to a mesh and a solver case

A CAD Model feeds the meshing tools directly, and its faces come along as named side sets:

  • Overlay Mesh of a CAD Model fills it with hexahedra; the mesh has one side set per CAD face (Bore side, Base z+, or plate_with_hole face 3 for a solid read from a STEP file), so a boundary condition can name the wall of a hole. Extract Set shows one.
  • In geometry commands, import 'part.step' (also .stp, .igs, .3mf, .sc, .afxcad) brings the part in as a body whose faces keep their names, and mesh body 1 size 0.5 scheme overlay meshes it; the Hex mesh output carries the same side sets.
  • Tet Mesh keeps the names too: one side set per CAD face.
  • Assign Grains and the solver cases follow as for any mesh: give the set to hold and the set to pull by name (fixed='plate_with_hole face 1', loaded='plate_with_hole face 2').

part = afx.load("bracket.step")                # a CAD Model
mesh = part.overlay_mesh(cell=1.0)             # hexahedra; side sets named after the faces
grains = mesh.assign_grains(grains=40, blocks=True, boundaries=True)
afx.simulation_case(grains, "moose", "case", fixed="bracket face 1", loaded="bracket face 2")

Every call is one undo step, and is recorded in the Python trace. A feature name can be used once (faces are named after features): adding a second Base is refused with that reason, and m.cad.clear("Name") empties a part to start from nothing.

Files

A part is saved as an .afxcad file: readable JSON with a format version, so a later auraFX opens an earlier file, and an earlier one says plainly that a file is from a newer version. The state files (.afx) hold the part inside, so a session reopens with it.

Meshing a part

A CAD model feeds the same tools as Geometry: Overlay Mesh turns its surface into hexahedra, Tet Mesh into tetrahedra, Assign Grains and the solver cases follow. Face names carry through: an extracted set or a boundary layer can name Bore side.

Limits

  • The Implicit kernel describes the part as a distance field. It never fails on a hard combination of features, but edges are not exact lines: the faces it reports are labelled regions of the surface. The B-rep kernel is exact but refuses a few things (a feature-level mirror of a shape that is not symmetric, a twisted sweep): Auto then falls back and says so.
  • A sketch follows its face by the face’s name: renaming the feature that made the face, or removing it, leaves the sketch without a face until you pick another. The face must be one the features before the sketch’s feature make, and flat.
  • A sweep’s path is one open chain of lines and arcs in one sketch (the longest, when a sketch has several); a closed path, guide rails and a profile that changes along the path are not there. A loft is straight between its sections, which must be on parallel planes.
  • Mates read a face’s plane or axis from its triangles. On the exact kernel that is exact; on the implicit kernel a small round face may not be recognised as round (the mate then says so). Angle mates run from 0 to 180°. There are no gear, cam or limit mates, and no collision while dragging (Check fit finds overlaps afterwards).
  • A component is edited on its own in its own coordinates; the rest of the assembly is not shown beside it, and a component’s own components are not opened from there.
  • A dimension that makes a sketch impossible (constraints that contradict each other) is refused, and the part keeps its last valid state.
  • Sketches start on the principal planes, on reference planes and on flat faces; a curved face cannot be sketched on.
  • Check fit samples the two solids on a grid, so a thin overlap smaller than a cell is missed; the result is accurate to a few percent for overlaps that span several cells.
