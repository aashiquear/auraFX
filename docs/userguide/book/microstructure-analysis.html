Measuring and simulating microstructures

The filters under Filters › Microstructure read a microstructure, say what it is like in numbers, and compute what it does: how much of each phase there is and how it is arranged, whether a phase runs through the sample, how stiff and how conducting the whole is, and how large a sample must be for those numbers to be trusted. They work on the types of Synthetic microstructures and on any other image with a phase for every cell: a segmented tomography, a map of phases, the result of Threshold on a volume.

This chapter also covers four sources of the Synthetic Microstructure window that run the models rather than being one: starting points that stand for a material, a model followed while it forms, a model run over many sizes and seeds, and a unit cell found for its stiffness or conductivity.

What the filters read

Every filter reads a phase for every cell of an image:

  • Phases (advanced): the cell array that holds the phases. Empty, it is the array named Phases. Any whole-number cell array will do (a FeatureIds array, a segmentation).
  • Phase: the number of the phase to measure. The synthetic types use 1 for the matrix or the pores and 2 for the solid.
  • Through periodic faces: on for a structure that was made periodic, so that what leaves through a face is counted as coming back through the opposite one.

A filter that gives numbers gives a table (open it in the Spreadsheet, or plot it); the numbers that sum up a result are field values, shown in the Information panel and read from Python with node.array("Name", "field").

Fractions, surface and connectivity

Phase Measures gives a table with a row for every phase:

ColumnMeaning
VolumeFractionThe share of the cells.
SpecificSurfaceThe surface of the phase per unit volume, from the boundaries met along lines in 13 directions (in a plane: the length of the outline per unit area, from 4 directions).
MeanChordThe mean length of the straight stretches through the phase: 4 times the fraction over the surface.
EulerNumberThe connected parts, less the tunnels, plus the closed cavities (in a plane: the parts less the holes). 1 for a ball, 0 for a ring, 2 for a hollow ball. A large negative number: a network with many loops.
EulerNumberPerVolumeThe same per unit volume.
ConnectedPartsThe number of separate parts (cells joined across faces).
ExpectedFraction, ExpectedSpecificSurfaceWhat the model gives exactly, where it kept it: the fraction of overlapping spheres and fibres, their surface and that of fibres on lines and of Gaussian fields.
 
s = afx.overlapping_spheres(radius=6, fraction=0.3)
t = s.phase_measures(periodic=True)

The surface of a round shape drawn on cells is found to within about 5 % when the shape is a few cells across; thin features read low.

Two-point statistics

Two-Point Statistics follows a phase against the distance between two points:

  • S2: the probability that both points lie in the phase, over all directions, and along each axis as S2 X, S2 Y, S2 Z. At no distance it is the volume fraction; far apart, the fraction squared; how fast it falls tells the size of the features, and a difference between the axes tells an anisotropy.
  • LinealPath X (and Y, Z): the probability that the whole segment between the points lies in the phase.
  • Cluster X (and Y, Z): the probability that the points lie in the same connected part.
  • Expected: the exact curve of the model, for overlapping spheres of one radius and for Gaussian field media.

The field value IntegralRange is the volume (the area in a plane) over which the phase is correlated with itself: a sample many times larger holds many independent pieces. Farthest distance (0: half the box) limits the table.

t = s.two_point_statistics(phase=2, periodic=True, reach=24)
t.plot("S2", "Expected", x="Distance")

Chords, thickness and pore size

Phase Chord Lengths gives the lengths of the straight stretches of a phase along lines in X, Y and Z, as the share of the chords in each class of length (Classes), with their means as the field value MeanChord. Chords cut by the box are left out.

Local Thickness gives, at every cell of a phase, the diameter of the largest ball that fits inside the phase and holds the cell (LocalThickness): the thickness of struts and walls for the solid, the size of the pores for phase 1. The field values MeanThickness and LargestThickness sum it up. With Result Size distribution the filter gives a table instead: the share of the phase in each class of thickness, and the share thinner than each size (what openings by balls of growing size would remove, one after another).

foam = afx.open_cell_foam(fraction=0.15)
struts = foam.local_thickness(phase=2)                 # LocalThickness at every cell
pores = foam.local_thickness(phase=1, output="Size distribution")

The balls are centred on cells, so thicknesses are right to about one cell; the faces of the box do not count as a boundary of the phase.

Paths, fabric and percolation

Tortuosity finds the shortest path inside a phase from one face of the box (From the face at the start of X, Y or Z) to every cell, kept as PathLength (-1 where no path leads). Tortuosity is the mean length of the paths that reach the opposite face over the straight distance, ShortestPath the same for the shortest one, ReachedShare the share of the opposite face that is reached. Paths go from cell to cell through faces, edges and corners, so they come out a few percent longer than a free path would.

Fabric Tensor tells along which directions a phase is long and short. It measures the mean chord along 13 directions and fits an ellipsoid to them; the table has a row for each axis of the ellipsoid (the mean chord along it, its share, its direction), and DegreeOfAnisotropy is the longest over the shortest. Trabecular bone and stretched foams give values well above 1; a structure with no preferred direction gives 1.

Percolation numbers the connected parts of a phase from the largest (ClusterIds), and says whether one of them joins opposite faces of the box: Percolates holds 1 or 0 for X, Y and Z, SpanningShare the share of the phase in such parts, Clusters their number and LargestClusterShare the share of the largest. Cells are joined across faces, faces and edges, or corners too.

p = s.percolation(phase=2)
print(p.array("Percolates", "field"))                      # [1, 1, 1]: the spheres join every pair of faces
paths = s.tortuosity(phase=1, axis="Z")                # through the pores

How large a sample

Representative Volume answers the question from one large image. It cuts the box into blocks of growing size and follows the variance of the mean of a property over the blocks against their volume. The variance falls as the variance of a single point times (range / volume) to a power; the fit gives the IntegralRange, the Exponent (1 for a structure without long-range order) and:

  • RepresentativeVolume and RepresentativeEdge: the volume, and the edge of the cube, that bring the mean to the Relative precision asked with the number of Samples given (several smaller samples do as well as one large);
  • PrecisionOfThisVolume: the relative precision this image itself gives.

Property empty measures the volume fraction of the phase. A cell array measures its mean: a component of the stress or of the flux from the property filters below gives the size needed for a stiffness or a conductivity, which is larger than the one the fraction needs.

big = afx.overlapping_spheres(cells=[128, 128, 128], radius=4, fraction=0.3)
r = big.representative_volume(phase=2, precision=0.01)
print(r.array("RepresentativeEdge", "field"))

Particle sizes

Split Touching Particles tells apart the particles of a phase that touch. Every cell of the phase goes to the particle whose core it leads to, the cores being the places farthest from the boundary; the particles are numbered in FeatureIds, so the feature filters of Grains and crystals (sizes, shapes, centroids, neighbours) measure them. Least neck depth decides when two cores are one particle: when the neck between them is less than this narrower, in radius, than the smaller core. Raise it if round particles come out cut in pieces.

Unfold Section Sizes gives the sizes of spheres in space from the sizes of their sections by a plane, as measured on a micrograph. A sphere shows a section of any size up to its own, so the largest sections count the largest spheres, and what those add to the smaller classes is taken off class after class. Give the Section diameters (a column of a table, or an array: the equivalent diameters from Feature Sizes on a section), the number of Classes and the Area of the section. The table has, for every class of diameter, the sections per unit area and the spheres per unit volume; the field values SpheresPerVolume, MeanDiameter and VolumeFraction sum it up. The method assumes spheres; few sections in a class make the result uneven.

Stretching and rebuilding

Stretch Microstructure stretches a structure by a factor along each of three axes (Stretch along the axes), the axes turned by Bunge angles if wanted: elongated grains and pores, aligned ellipsoids from spheres, a field correlated farther one way. The result is on the same grid. With The structure is periodic on, what comes in from beyond the box is the structure repeated; off, its mirror image. A stretched structure no longer joins up through the faces it was stretched along, and it no longer keeps the description of its objects.

Reconstruct Microstructure makes a new random medium, of any size, with the statistics of a phase of the input: its volume fraction, its two-point probability along each axis and, with Match the lineal path too, the lengths of its straight stretches. Cells of the phase are swapped with cells outside it again and again, a swap kept when it brings the statistics nearer (simulated annealing); Swaps tried per cell sets how long it runs. From a single layer of cells (a micrograph) it makes a volume, the third axis taking the mean of the two measured ones. The result is periodic. Two media with the same two-point probability can look different: the copy is a statistical one.

long = s.stretch_microstructure(stretch=[1, 1, 2])
copy = s.reconstruct_microstructure(phase=2, cells=[48, 48, 48], sweeps=20)

Exact geometry and smooth surfaces

The types made of objects keep a description of them with the image: the spheres of the particle types, the pieces of the fibres, the struts of lattices, foams and networks, the pipes and the branches. Two filters draw from it.

Microstructure Objects gives the objects as geometry: spheres as points, fibre pieces and struts as lines, each with its Radius (and Length for the lines). Glyph with spheres scaled by Radius and Tube with a radius from Radius draw them exactly; the points and lines are also what a beam model or a count of objects needs.

Smooth Surface gives the closed surface of a phase as triangles, without the steps of the cells. For a type that kept its objects, the surface is that of the exact spheres, round-ended fibres and struts, drawn Resolution times finer than the image. For any other image (a field, a foam drawn from distances, a tomography), the steps of the cells are smoothed over Smoothing cells. The surface is closed where the box cuts the phase. Save it as STL to print it, or give it to Tet Mesh for a mesh of tetrahedra that follows the surface.

lattice = afx.strut_lattice(cell="Octet", fraction=0.15)
skin = lattice.microstructure_surface(refine=3)
skin.save("octet.stl")

Hollow fibres and struts, flat fibre ends, coatings and combinations are not in the description: turn Always from the cells on for those.

Framework Rigidity takes the struts of a lattice as bars joined by pins and counts, from the rank of the matrix that gives the stretch of every bar from the movements of the nodes:

ColumnMeaning
Nodes, Struts, StrutsPerNodeThe frame.
MaxwellNumberStruts less the freedoms of the nodes: the states of self-stress less the mechanisms.
MechanismsThe ways the frame can move without stretching a strut.
StatesOfSelfStressThe ways its struts can be stressed with no load.
FloppyStrainsFor a periodic lattice: the mean strains (of six) it cannot resist by stretching its struts.

A lattice with no floppy strain carries loads by stretching its struts and is stiff for its weight (the octet truss); one with floppy strains bends its struts instead (the cubic lattice in shear, the Kelvin cell). On a Disordered Strut Lattice the count shows where removing struts makes the lattice lose its stiffness. Large lattices are refused: analyse a few cells.

Conductivity and stiffness

Two filters compute the properties of the microstructure as a whole from those of its phases. The box is taken as one period of an endless medium; a mean gradient (or strain) is applied and the field found everywhere by an iterative solver that works in Fourier space. Make the structure periodic when you can; a structure that is not is still computed, with its faces joined as they come.

Effective Conductivity (heat, electricity, diffusion) takes the Conductivity of each phase, in the order of the phases (phase 1 first; the last value serves for the phases beyond). It keeps:

  • EffectiveConductivity: the nine values of the tensor (the flux along each axis for a unit gradient along each axis);
  • ConductivityBounds: four values the result must lie between, from the fractions alone: the phases in series, the Hashin-Shtrikman lower and upper bounds, the phases in parallel;
  • TortuosityFactor, along each axis: by how much the paths through the best conductor are longer and narrower than straight ones (its fraction times its conductivity over the effective one);
  • the cell arrays Gradient and Flux for the gradient along one axis (Keep the fields of the gradient along).

Effective Stiffness takes a Young’s modulus and a Poisson’s ratio for each phase. Give pores a small modulus (a thousandth of the solid) rather than 0. It keeps:

  • EffectiveStiffness: the 36 values of the stiffness matrix, rows and columns in the order xx, yy, zz, yz, xz, xy, with engineering shear strains;
  • EffectiveModuli (Young’s moduli along X, Y, Z, shear moduli YZ, XZ, XY) and PoissonRatios (XY, XZ, YZ);
  • BulkModulus and ShearModulus, the means over the directions, with BulkBounds and ShearBounds (Reuss, Hashin-Shtrikman lower and upper, Voigt), and AnisotropyRatio (1 when the stiffness is the same in every direction);
  • the cell arrays Strain, Stress (six components in the same order) and VonMisesStress for one of the six loads (Keep the fields of).
s = afx.hard_spheres(radius=6, fraction=0.3)
e = s.effective_stiffness(youngs=[1, 20], poisson=[0.3, 0.2], load="Stretch along X")
print(e.array("EffectiveModuli", "field"))
e.set(color_by="VonMisesStress")
k = s.effective_conductivity(conductivities=[1, 50])

The solvers need cubic cells. A grid of 64 cells along each edge takes from seconds to a minute; the time grows with the contrast between the phases, and Iterations at most and Tolerance (advanced) bound it: SolverResidual says how far the solver got. The properties are those of this one sample; see Representative Volume for how far they are from those of the material.

Waves and band gaps

Wave Band Structure gives the frequencies of the elastic waves that can travel through a structure repeated in every direction, along a path of wave vectors: from the centre of the zone of one period to the middle of a face, of an edge, to a corner and back. Give each phase its Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio and Density. The table has the distance along the path (Path), the wave vector and one column per band; plotted against Path they are the dispersion curves. A range of frequencies that no band crosses is a band gap, in which no wave gets through: BandGaps lists them (lower and upper frequency), PathStops where the corners of the path are.

cell = afx.hard_spheres(cells=[32, 32, 1], radius=10, fraction=0.3, method="Dense packing")
bands = cell.wave_band_structure(youngs=[1, 30], density=[1, 8], waves=4, bands=8)
bands.plot("Band 1", "Band 2", "Band 3", "Band 4", x="Path")

The displacement is written as a sum of plane waves, from minus to plus Plane waves each way along an axis along every axis. More waves give finer bands and take much longer (in a volume, 3 each way is already slow); with a strong contrast between the phases the higher bands are indicative only, and voids must be given a small stiffness and a small density rather than none. Frequencies are in cycles per unit time for the units of the moduli, the densities and the cell size.

Unit cells found for a property

Optimized Unit Cell (Synthetic Microstructure › Lattices and foams) finds a periodic unit cell rather than drawing one: every cell holds a density of material between 0 and 1, the property of the periodic medium and what each cell contributes to it are computed, and material is moved to where it serves most, step after step, at a constant Volume fraction. Best chooses the property: the Bulk modulus (stiffness in compression from all sides), the Shear modulus or the Conductivity. The result has Phases like every type and the Density it was cut from; the value reached (BulkModulus, ShearModulus or Conductivity, with the solid’s own property as unit) is kept beside the UpperBound that no structure of that fraction can pass.

  • Smoothing radius sets the least size of a member, in cells.
  • Start from A soft centre gives cells with a void in the middle; Random a few soft places at random (other seeds, other cells).
  • One layer of cells along Z designs a cell in the plane.
cell = afx.optimized_cell(goal="Bulk modulus", fraction=0.3, cells=[32, 32, 32], iterations=40)
print(cell.array("BulkModulus", "field"), cell.array("UpperBound", "field"))

The search stops at a good cell, not always the best one, and a coarse grid limits how thin its walls can be. It takes a minute or so for 32 cells along each edge.

From Python and the command palette the same are Microstructure Template:

bone = afx.microstructure_template(template="Trabecular bone")
wood = afx.microstructure_template(template="Softwood", cells=[128, 96, 48])

Watching a structure form

Microstructure Formation (Synthetic Microstructure › Sequences and studies) follows a model while it forms, as time steps to play with the animation controls: grains that nucleate and grow, an automaton or a pattern step after step, an aggregate particle after particle, a network ligament after ligament.

  • Model: Nucleation and Growth Grains, Cellular Automaton Structure, Activator-Inhibitor Pattern, Reaction-Diffusion Pattern, Aggregation Cluster or Scale-Rich Network.
  • Settings of the model: the settings that differ from the standard ones, written as name = value; name = value with the names of Python (cells = 96 96 1; seed = 3).
  • Time steps, and From, to: the first and last values of what advances the model (the time, the number of steps, the fraction, the number of ligaments); empty, the standard ones.
f = afx.microstructure_formation(model="Nucleation and Growth Grains", frames=24,
                                 settings="cells = 128 128 1; rate = 0.0005")
afx.save_animation("growth.mp4")

Every time step makes the model again up to that stage, so a step takes as long as the model does.

Studies over sizes and seeds

Microstructure Study (Synthetic Microstructure › Sequences and studies) runs a model again and again and gives a table, a row for every size of the box and every value of one setting:

  • Model and Settings of the model as above;
  • Setting to vary (the Python name of one setting, or empty) and Its values;
  • Cells along an edge: one box size or several;
  • Samples of each: the number of seeds;
  • Phase and how its cells are joined.

The columns are the mean and the variance of the volume fraction of the phase (MeanFraction, VarianceOfFraction), the IntegralRange the variance gives, the SpecificSurface, and the share of the samples in which the phase joins opposite faces (PercolatesX, PercolatesY, PercolatesZ, and Percolates for any of them). With a setting varied, the field value Thresholds holds, for every size, the value at which half the samples percolate, and ThresholdOfAnEndlessBox carries them to an endless box.

t = afx.microstructure_study(model="Overlapping Spheres", settings="radius = 3",
                             vary="fraction", values=[0.2, 0.24, 0.28, 0.32, 0.36, 0.4],
                             sizes=[40, 56], realizations=10)
print(t.array("Thresholds", "field"))                      # near 0.29 for overlapping spheres

A study makes sizes x values x samples structures, up to 5000: start small. The threshold needs values on both sides of it and enough samples for the shares to be smooth.

Limits

  • The measures read cells: a feature under about three cells across is not resolved, and surfaces and thicknesses are right to a few percent or a cell.
  • The property filters, the optimized cell and the band structure need cubic cells and take the box as periodic.
  • Wave bands with voids, and stiffness with a modulus of 0, are not solved: give the voids small values.
  • Framework Rigidity counts pin-jointed bars; it says nothing of the stiffness the bending of the struts adds.
  • Unfold Section Sizes assumes spheres.
